## Captured excerpts

What Microsoft Copilot (free) includes:

> "Microsoft Copilot is available at no cost at copilot.cloud.microsoft"

> It "understands your questions and requests, provides direct answers, assists with writing, and even creates images"

> Signing in with a personal account "gives you access to chat history, image creation, longer conversations, voice interactions"

Capabilities listed for free:

> "Chat with AI for help with writing, brainstorming, summarizing, and more."
> "Get real-time answers based on web search."
> "Use Copilot in Edge for webpage summarization and insights."

Who it is framed for:

> "ideal for general questions and answers, people trying AI for the first time, and web-based tasks"

Access points:

> "web browser, desktop or mobile apps, … or use directly in Copilot in Edge" (Windows, macOS, iOS, Android per the article)

What paid individual plans add:

> Desktop apps include "deep integration with documents, emails, spreadsheets, calendars and more"

> "Subscription owners of Microsoft 365 Personal, Family, or Premium have access to Copilot in the" desktop apps like Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, OneNote

> Premium gets exclusive "Advanced AI features"; "Agents like Tasks, Analyst, and Researcher."; and "Extended usage allowance"

Limitations (the page has no section titled "limitations of the free version"; these are the closest statements):

> Free-tier scope is framed as "general questions and answers, people trying AI for the first time, and web-based tasks" — no desktop-app or work-data integration is listed for free.

> "Sign in isn't required, but signing in with your personal account gives you access to chat history, image creation, longer conversations"

> "Copilot features, functionality, and availability may vary by region."

Privacy (paid plans only; no explicit free-tier privacy claim on the page):

> Business subscribers get "built-in IT controls, enterprise data protection, and agent management"
